Finding a focus

The collection spans disciplines and contains titles, abstracts, programs, years, and dissertation-language information. English dominated the dissertation-language field; Spanish was the largest non-English category, with 215 dissertations. Of the 209 Spanish-language dissertations with abstracts, 196 abstracts were written in English and 13 in Spanish. I modeled the 196 English-language abstracts so the texts would be comparable.

From text to topics

I cleaned and preprocessed the 196 English-language abstracts in Python, removed stopwords, stemmed terms, and represented documents as bags of words. I used Latent Dirichlet Allocation to identify recurring combinations of terms, then examined prominent terms alongside the dissertations most strongly associated with each topic. I interpreted the five areas as translation and textual tradition; subjectivity, politics, and narrative; literature, history, and political change; culture, politics, and nation; and Spanish language and sociolinguistics.

Reading the model

The network connects each theme to its prominent terms. Shared words stay connected to every relevant theme; thicker links and larger word nodes reflect their relative weights in the model. Select a topic to see its primary dissertation count and examples, or select a word to see where themes overlap. These labels are interpretations developed from model output and abstracts, not definitive classifications. The network describes patterns in these 196 English-language abstracts of Spanish-language dissertations; it does not measure trends across all nearly 19,000 records.
